About Nir

I like businesses.
I like bodies.
I like difficult questions.

And, increasingly, I like what happens when we stop pretending they belong in separate conversations.

Nir, relaxed, island context

The short version

Systems, logic, and how things work. Then people.

I was born in Israel and grew up in South Africa. I studied electronic engineering because I genuinely like systems, logic and understanding how things work. Later came an MBA, years in strategy, marketing and consulting, building companies, advising founders and working inside businesses where decisions have actual consequences.


I also became a musician. Not as a hobby tucked politely after work. Music became another way of understanding people. I’ve played and facilitated experiences where a room can move from guarded to open without anyone explaining a framework first.

The business side

I still do real commercial work. I like that.

My career has moved through technology, consulting, marketing, entrepreneurship and leadership. I’ve worked with founders and organisations on positioning, offers, marketing, operations, teams and growth. I co-founded a startup and learned the less glamorous version of entrepreneurship too: raising money, building a team, making bets, getting things wrong, and discovering that knowing a lot about business does not exempt you from being human.


It keeps this practice attached to the world where someone eventually has to choose a price, ship the page, make the hire or have the conversation.

Nir, business / work context

Then there’s the part that doesn’t fit neatly on LinkedIn

My own nervous system has not been a theoretical interest.

Childhood experiences, health and body pain, intimacy, attachment, work, control, achievement and relationships have all met each other in fairly inconvenient ways.


I’ve spent years in trauma-aware and somatic work, including inner-child work, embodiment, nervous-system practice and relational work. I’ve also facilitated workshops and experiences in these worlds.


I don’t use my story to claim that everyone’s business problem is childhood trauma. It isn’t. But I know from the inside what it is like when an intelligent adult understands something perfectly and another part of them is still protecting an older reality.

Guitar, on stage or mid-performance

Music is part of this too

What sound can do before language gets involved.

I’m an award-winning fingerstyle guitarist and I’ve used music in concerts, ecstatic-dance spaces and inner journeys. I’m fascinated by what sound can do before language gets involved: activation, settling, emotion, connection, permission.


That part of me belongs here. Not because your pricing strategy needs a guitar solo, but because I don’t think humans become more useful when we cut them into business, body, relationship and creativity.

Used daily. Not a bonus module.

And then I became slightly obsessed with AI.

I use AI every day. To think, research, challenge ideas, build systems, turn conversations into useful material, create content, rehearse difficult conversations and get work out of my head and into the world.


I love it enough that I want to work with people who are curious about it too. You don’t need to be technical. You do need to be willing to use a capable paid AI tool as a serious working partner.


The interesting question for me is not AI versus humanity. It is: what should the machine carry, so the human can be more present for the part only a human can do?

Regulated Strategy

It came from putting the lives back together.

Business strategy without the person can become a beautiful plan nobody executes.

Nervous-system work without the business can create insight without movement.

AI without judgement can produce an extraordinary amount of very polished avoidance.

I want all three in the room.

Make the strategy better. Notice what happens when it becomes real. Use technology to reduce unnecessary friction. Then take a step that matters.
