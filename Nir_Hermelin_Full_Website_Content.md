## The interesting bit is usually between knowing and doing.

You can understand that you should raise the price and still rewrite the proposal six times. You can know you need to delegate and somehow keep becoming the bottleneck. You can have a perfectly sensible content plan and publish nothing for a year.

I know that last one personally.

The question isn’t automatically, “What is wrong with my nervous system?” Sometimes the offer is weak. Sometimes the plan is unrealistic. Sometimes you need a better system. And sometimes the move carries more threat, meaning or history than the spreadsheet can see.

Good work is figuring out which is which.

## Interactive: What pressure can look like at work

**Keep the current interactive map, but update its framing.** These are useful lenses, not a diagnostic and not a fixed ladder. Do not plot fawn as simply another point on one activation curve.

**REGULATED / CONNECTED**  
Useful: perspective, curiosity, flexible attention, listening.  
Watch for: calm is not the goal all day. Business also needs energy, mobilisation and healthy stress.

**FIGHT / MOBILISE**  
Useful: boundaries, decisive action, protecting a priority, saying no.  
Watch for: control, irritability, forcing, turning every disagreement into a contest.

**FLIGHT / MOBILISE**  
Useful: speed, scanning, problem-solving, spotting opportunities.  
Watch for: compulsive busyness, difficulty stopping, solving tomorrow while today is still happening.

**FREEZE / SHUTDOWN**  
Can look like blankness, avoidance, fatigue, difficulty choosing or starting.  
Useful question: is this protection, depletion, confusion, a bad plan, or some combination?

**FAWN / SOCIAL PROTECTION**  
Can look like over-accommodating, softening the price before anyone objects, saying yes while resenting it, managing everyone else’s comfort. Treat it as a relational protection strategy that can coexist with different levels of activation.

# REGULATED STRATEGY / METHOD

**REGULATED STRATEGY**

# A business method that includes the person running the business.

Seven lenses I use to move from a messy business problem to a real experiment, without assuming the answer is either “push harder” or “regulate more.”

## Small enough for the nervous system. Real enough for the business.

The goal is not permanent calm. Business needs activation. It needs risk, visibility, decisions and difficult conversations.

The question is whether we can find a useful edge: enough challenge to create new evidence and capacity, not so much force that you disappear, flood yourself or spend the next week recovering.

### 01 · Business Reality
What is actually happening? Revenue, pipeline, offer, workload, team, customer evidence, constraints. Before we explain the problem psychologically, we make sure it is a good business problem.

### 02 · Relationship to the Issue
What changes in you when we get close to this particular move? More urgency? Fog? A sudden need to research? An urge to make everyone happy? This is observation, not diagnosis.

### 03 · Somatic Mapping
If it is useful, we bring attention to the body. Sensation can give us information that the story is skipping. We stay curious about it rather than treating every tight chest as proof of a specific trauma response.

### 04 · Protective Logic
What might this response be trying to protect? Belonging, certainty, control, reputation, rest, safety from conflict? Sometimes an old pattern is relevant. Sometimes today’s situation is genuinely badly designed. We test both.

### 05 · Conditions for Movement
What would make the next attempt more workable? Better information, a script, rehearsal, a smaller audience, a clearer boundary, recovery time, an AI-generated first draft, someone beside you? This is where strategy and regulation start becoming practical.

### 06 · Strategic Stretch
Then we do something real. Send it. Ask. Publish. Hold the price. Delegate the piece. Have the conversation. The move is deliberately meaningful enough to teach us something.

### 07 · Integrate the Learning
What happened? What did the market tell us? What did your system tell us? What should become a policy, prompt, template, rhythm or AI workflow so next time requires less heroism?

## AI belongs inside the method.
It can help us get from activation to action without pretending it can regulate for you. We use it to make drafts, compare options, role-play buyers, analyse transcripts, build scripts, prepare meetings and turn a useful decision into a repeatable system.

But AI can also become avoidance with better grammar. At some point a real person has to see the offer.

## Stretch. Recover. Integrate. Repeat.
Think of training, lightly. Growth usually needs some load. Load without recovery is not the same thing as adaptation. The metaphor has limits, but I like the rhythm: approach an edge, come back, learn, then approach again.

We are not trying to win an award for tolerating discomfort. We are trying to expand what you can do while keeping the business commercially honest.

## FAQ
**Is this therapy?**  
No. We work on business decisions and behaviour. Personal history and nervous-system responses may be relevant, but this is not clinical treatment. If something belongs in therapy, we treat that boundary seriously.

**Do I have to be “dysregulated”?**  
No. You do need to be interested in the nervous-system lens. This is part of the work, not a hidden marketing device.

**Do I need to be good at AI?**  
No. Curiosity is enough. Resistance to using it is probably a bad fit for these programmes.

**Will you guarantee sales?**  
No. We can improve the offer, the materials and the quality and speed of testing. Buyers remain inconveniently independent humans.

**Can we just do strategy?**  
We can start there. If the problem is strategic, good. If the same pattern keeps appearing when the strategy becomes real, we have another useful place to look.
