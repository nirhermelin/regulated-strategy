Regulated Strategy

A business method that includes the person running the business.

Seven lenses I use to move from a messy business problem to a real experiment, without assuming the answer is either “push harder” or “regulate more.”

Small enough for the nervous system.
Real enough for the business.

The goal is not permanent calm. Business needs activation. It needs risk, visibility, decisions and difficult conversations.

The question is whether we can find a useful edge: enough challenge to create new evidence and capacity, not so much force that you disappear, flood yourself or spend the next week recovering.

The seven lenses

From messy problem to real experiment.

Business Reality

What is actually happening? Revenue, pipeline, offer, workload, team, customer evidence, constraints. Before we explain the problem psychologically, we make sure it is a good business problem.

Relationship to the Issue

What changes in you when we get close to this particular move? More urgency? Fog? A sudden need to research? An urge to make everyone happy?

This is observation, not diagnosis.

Somatic Mapping

If it is useful, we bring attention to the body. Sensation can give us information that the story is skipping. We stay curious about it rather than treating every tight chest as proof of a specific trauma response.

Protective Logic

What might this response be trying to protect? Belonging, certainty, control, reputation, rest, safety from conflict? Sometimes an old pattern is relevant. Sometimes today’s situation is genuinely badly designed.

We test both.

Conditions for Movement

What would make the next attempt more workable? Better information, a script, rehearsal, a smaller audience, a clearer boundary, recovery time, an AI-generated first draft, someone beside you?

This is where strategy and regulation start becoming practical.

Strategic Stretch

Then we do something real. Send it. Ask. Publish. Hold the price. Delegate the piece. Have the conversation. The move is deliberately meaningful enough to teach us something.

Integrate the Learning

What happened? What did the market tell us? What did your system tell us? What should become a policy, prompt, template, rhythm or AI workflow so next time requires less heroism?

AI belongs inside the method

From activation to action, without pretending it can regulate for you.

We use it to make drafts, compare options, role-play buyers, analyse transcripts, build scripts, prepare meetings and turn a useful decision into a repeatable system.


But AI can also become avoidance with better grammar. At some point a real person has to see the offer.

Where it helps
  • First drafts, so the blank page isn’t the stretch
  • Rehearsing the buyer conversation before the real one
  • Comparing options you’re too close to
  • Turning a decision into a template or workflow
Where it doesn’t
  • It can’t regulate for you
  • Its feedback is not customer evidence
  • Another prompt can be a very polished way of not sending it

The rhythm

Stretch.Recover.Integrate.Repeat.

Stretch. Recover. Integrate. Repeat.

Think of training, lightly. Growth usually needs some load. Load without recovery is not the same thing as adaptation. The metaphor has limits, but I like the rhythm: approach an edge, come back, learn, then approach again.


We are not trying to win an award for tolerating discomfort. We are trying to expand what you can do while keeping the business commercially honest.
